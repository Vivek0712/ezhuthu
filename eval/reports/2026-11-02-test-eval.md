# Full TEST evaluation (2026-11-02)

> **TEST split, scored once** (issue #26). Every strategy, threshold and calibration was chosen on VAL first
> ([`2026-11-02-val-selection.md`](2026-11-02-val-selection.md)); nothing below was tuned on these items. "Matches the
> expert restoration" means the fill equals the DHARMA editor's supplied text after ISO normalisation; it does not
> mean "correct". Gap lengths are counted in Tamil letters throughout (the issue #26 fix); every earlier TEST number
> in `eval/reports/` is superseded by this report.

## Headline

- **The shipped pipeline** (routed Sonnet 4.6 -> Opus 4.6, 2B decider `noul-topk:3`, ontology on): top-1 matches
  the expert on **205 of 413** test gaps (**0.4964**, 95% CI 0.4479-0.5448). The copy-from-best-parallel baseline
  scores 0.4722; the difference is not significant (38 vs 28 discordant gaps, p = 0.27).
- **The character 5-gram baseline is the best restorer on TEST**: 0.5714 (0.5206-0.6199), above copy (p = 0.0003)
  and above the shipped pipeline (77 vs 46 discordant gaps, p = 0.0066). 68% of TEST gaps are a single letter (VAL:
  46%), where the 5-gram is strongest (0.6868); on gaps of 4+ letters (n = 51) every system is at 0.10-0.33.
- **Abstention:** at its VAL-fitted thresholds the shipped pipeline fills 46.5% of gaps and 63.5% of those fills
  match the expert (122 of 192); ECE 0.113.
- **Opus helps, Sonnet alone does not:** routed beats Sonnet-only (0.4165 -> 0.4964; 43 vs 10, p < 0.0001); Sonnet
  alone is below copy (p = 0.006). Opus 4.6 alone on a seeded 100-gap sample: 0.50 (routed on the same 100: 0.49).
- **The 2B decider does not beat prerank for restoration ranking on TEST:** on the same LLM candidates, decider vs
  prerank is 27 vs 28 discordant gaps for Sonnet (p = 1.0) and 25 vs 29 for routed (p = 0.68); on retrieval-only
  candidates the best VAL decider variant (`choice-all`) is below prerank (46 vs 72, p = 0.021). The hosted v19
  decider (decider-cloud) is **not measured**.
- **Ontology ablation:** no measurable effect. 88 of 413 gaps get an ontology hint; with it on vs off, Sonnet's top-1
  matches on 3 gaps only with it and 3 only without (p = 1.0).
- **Cost and latency (shipped routed pipeline):** 122 Sonnet + 300 Opus calls for 413 gaps, about $2.50 at list
  prices ($0.006 per gap); 299 of 413 gaps use Opus (291 by pre-escalation). Latency p50 8.2 s per gap, p90 12.2 s.

## How this run went (read before re-using the rows)

- Live Bedrock calls for the whole issue: about 1,075 (1,075 distinct cached replies: VAL about 240, TEST about 835),
  within the 1,200 budget. Account quotas are Sonnet 4.6 10 and Opus 4.6 5 requests a minute, so the TEST run warmed
  the reply cache per model in parallel lanes (a cached reply is byte-identical to a live one for the restorer).
- A first TEST attempt was stopped after its retrieval-only rows (a lane made no progress); it was restarted from the
  same commit. Retrieval-only rows are deterministic and came out identical; its ~14 Sonnet replies were replayed.
- Throttling lost 7 of 413 Sonnet gaps and 2 of 88 ablation gaps in the first pass (8 retries each). They were
  replayed from the cache with no setting changed (`repair`, 0 live calls): the other 406 Sonnet rows were unchanged,
  1 of the other 86 ablation rows changed its top-1 (the local decider is not bit-exact between runs; the same
  happened on VAL, e.g. `choice-topk:3` 0.2370 vs 0.2393 on two full-VAL runs). The rows' meta records the lost ids.
- The two "prerank instead of the decider" rows were added after the main pass to answer the decider question
  above (8 more live Opus calls). They change no shipped setting.
- Not run: the E4B decider (the 2B decider at ~1 s per `noul` was already the bottleneck of this run) and the hosted
  v19 decider (no measurement in this repository).

## VAL vs TEST

The VAL choices did not all hold on TEST. On VAL (120-gap sample) the decider `noul-topk:3` beat self-report and
prerank on Sonnet candidates (0.1917 vs 0.1167 / 0.15); on TEST the decider and prerank are level. On full VAL the
5-gram baseline (0.2980) was already above copy (0.2415) and every retrieval strategy (best 0.2619). TEST has a much
larger share of single-letter gaps (68% vs 46%), so absolute numbers are not comparable across the two splits.
