# Reader (photo/file upload, `POST /api/read`): OCR evaluation, 2026-11-02

**Bottom line.** Photo reading is **experimental**. On Tamil-script text it is usable: character error rate (CER)
**0.097** on 72 synthetic plates rendered from DHARMA TEST-split lines (0.047 clean, 0.052 photo-like, 0.192
weathered), and a mean CER of **0.093 on 3 real printed Tamil pages**. On **Tamil-Brahmi it fails**: CER **0.83 to
0.91 on 3 real images** of the Jambai inscription and 0.80 on synthetic Tamil-Brahmi, with Opus no better. Grantha
(synthetic only) is at 0.56. **Vatteluttu is not measured at all**: it has no Unicode encoding or font, so nothing
could be rendered, and we found no openly licensed Vatteluttu photograph with a published reading. The app labels every
photo reading "experimental reading: check it against the edition" in Tamil and English, and the server adds a
warning when the script is Tamil-Brahmi, Vatteluttu or Grantha, or the reader's confidence is under 0.5.

Live Bedrock calls: **109 of the 150 budget** (88 for the main synthetic run, including a 2-call smoke test whose
replies were then reused from the cache; 7 for the real set; 14 for the Opus follow-up). Estimated cost about
**$1.06** at list prices. Everything is reproducible from the cache under `.cache/llm/`.

## What was measured, and how

| | Synthetic (a) | Real (b) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source text | 72 lines from 24 DHARMA **TEST-split** inscriptions (`benchmark/inscriptions.jsonl` `text_iso_masked`, one plate of 3 consecutive lines per inscription, round-robin over Pallava / Chola / Pandya / undated) | 3 images of one Tamil-Brahmi inscription (Jambai) and 3 printed pages of modern Tamil books |
| Images | Rendered by Chromium with the Noto fonts the app ships (Tamil sans and serif, Brahmi, Grantha) on a procedural stone texture; no photographs. 3 levels: **clean** (paper, sharp), **photo** (stone, carved shading, rotation up to 2.5 deg, perspective, blur 0.6 px, noise sigma 7, JPEG q70), **weathered** (stronger texture and perspective, blur 1.3 px, 6 erosion patches over letters, noise sigma 16, JPEG q45). Every plate in Tamil script at all 3 levels (72 images); 6 plates also in Tamil-Brahmi and Grantha at the photo level (12 images). | Wikimedia Commons and Tamil Wikisource, licences recorded in `eval/ocr/real_images.json` (CC BY-SA 3.0/4.0, CC0, public domain). Images are downloaded by `python -m ezhuthu.eval.ocr fetch-real`, not committed. |
| Reference | The edition line (lost text shown as `[?N]` gaps, drawn as chipped stretches) | Jambai: the reading after Mahadevan, *Early Tamil Epigraphy* (2003), as quoted on Wikipedia; published variants (itta/ītta, paḷi/pāḻi) are worth about 2 letters. Printed pages: the community-**validated** Wikisource page text, transliterated to ISO-15919. |
| Reader | `routed` (Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6 when confidence < 0.6 and the first read took < 10 s), exactly as deployed | same |

Scoring (`src/ezhuthu/eval/ocr.py`): CER is the Levenshtein distance between the letters only (ISO-15919
`normalise_for_scoring`: spaces, hyphens, gap markers and editorial marks dropped) divided by the reference letters,
summed over images (micro average). WER uses whitespace words in the same form. Gap markers are counted separately.

Commands (from the repo root, `app/web` needs `npm ci` for the renderer):

```bash
python -m ezhuthu.eval.ocr synth --plates 24 --seed 7
python -m ezhuthu.eval.ocr fetch-real
EZHUTHU_LIVE=1 python -m ezhuthu.eval.ocr run --set synth --max-live-calls 118 --out eval/reports/2026-11-02-ocr-eval-synth.jsonl
EZHUTHU_LIVE=1 python -m ezhuthu.eval.ocr run --set real  --max-live-calls 20  --out eval/reports/2026-11-02-ocr-eval-real.jsonl
python -m ezhuthu.eval.ocr report --synth eval/reports/2026-11-02-ocr-eval-synth.jsonl --real eval/reports/2026-11-02-ocr-eval-real.jsonl --out eval/reports/2026-11-02-ocr-eval
```

## (a) Synthetic results

Tamil script, all 72 images scored:

| Level | Images | CER vs edition | CER vs what the image shows | WER |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| clean | 24 | **0.047** | 0.037 | 0.259 |
| photo | 24 | **0.052** | 0.043 | 0.264 |
| weathered | 24 | **0.192** | 0.186 | 0.561 |
| all | 72 | **0.097** | 0.089 | 0.361 |

"What the image shows" is the edition
line rendered in Tamil script and transliterated back: Tamil script cannot show some ISO distinctions, so this is the
fairer target for a reader; the gap between the two columns (about 0.01) is that loss.

WER is much higher than CER because DHARMA divides words with hyphens and line-end splits (`yāṇ·` / `ṭu`) that a
reader of the stone cannot see; most "word errors" are one wrong letter or a different word division.

By the period of the edition (Tamil script, all levels; the letters are modern Noto forms in every case, so this is
about vocabulary and spelling, not period letter shapes):

| Dynasty | Images | CER |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pallava | 21 | 0.097 |
| Chola | 18 | 0.107 |
| Pandya | 12 | 0.133 |
| undated | 21 | 0.073 |

Other scripts, same 6 plates at the photo level:

| Rendered script | Images | CER (routed) | CER (Opus 4.6 only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tamil (same plates) | 6 | **0.040** | not run |
| Tamil-Brahmi (Unicode Brahmi, Noto Sans Brahmi) | 6 | **0.804** | 0.864 |
| Grantha (Noto Sans Grantha) | 5 of 6 scored (1 failed after 5 throttling retries) | **0.558** | 0.549 |
| Vatteluttu | not possible: no Unicode encoding or font | | |

Script identification on these renderings: Tamil script was called `modern-tamil` 72/72 (right: it is a modern
font). Tamil-Brahmi was called `vatteluttu` 3/6 and `modern-tamil` 3/6; Grantha `modern-tamil` 5/5. So on synthetic
plates the script guess is wrong for every non-Tamil rendering, although the reader's confidence on them was low
(0.10 to 0.40), which is what the app's warning keys on.

Gaps (`[?N]` in the edition, drawn as chipped stretches):

| Level | Gaps in the edition | Gaps the reader wrote | Recall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| clean | 9 | 0 | 0.00 |
| photo | 9 | 5 | 0.56 (all 5 at a real gap) |
| weathered | 9 | 46 | 0.89 |

On clean paper a gap is drawn as plain blank space, and the reader (correctly, for paper) does not call blank space
lost text. On weathered plates the extra gaps are mostly the erosion patches, which cover letters that the edition
still has, so "precision" there is not meaningful: marking them as gaps is the behaviour we want.

Confidence is informative: Tamil-script images with reader confidence under 0.6 had CER **0.295** (n = 9), those at
or above 0.6 had **0.073** (n = 63).

Escalation: 5 of 84 images were re-read by Opus 4.6. Their CER moved from 0.244 (Sonnet) to 0.240 (Opus) on average
(per image: 0.185 to 0.130, 0.210 to 0.220, 0.467 to 0.444, 0.139 to 0.111, 0.219 to 0.297), so **no measurable
gain**, on a tiny sample. 16 low-confidence images did not escalate because the first read had already taken over
10 s; in this run that time includes throttling back-off from 4 parallel workers. Per-call model latency from the
cache: Sonnet median 6.3 s, p90 11.6 s, max 16.0 s (85 calls); Opus median 7.1 s (5 calls). A single read plus one
escalation fits the proxy's 27 s; two slow calls may not, which is why escalation is time-gated and the app offers
"read again with the larger model" instead.

### Failure cases, shown honestly

- **Tamil-Brahmi is not read.** Reference `satiyaputo atiyaṉ neṭumāṉ añci ītta pāḻi`. Sonnet on the eye-copy drawing:
  `o ka ṭa ya ta ya ka ṭa ṭa i ca ca pa ṭa ya ca ś o ...`, letter salad. Worse, **Opus on the Tnexplore photo produced
  a fluent but invented reading**, `arupattaṉ kuṭi ceḻiyaṉ a kaṉṉaṉ`, which looks like a real Tamil-Brahmi donative
  phrase and is not on the stone. Its confidence was 0.25. This is the main risk of the feature, and the reason for
  the server warning on Tamil-Brahmi, Vatteluttu and Grantha.
- **The script is recognised even when the letters are not**: all 3 real Jambai images were called `tamil-brahmi`.
- **Opus sometimes abstains completely** on synthetic Tamil-Brahmi (`[?12] / [?10] / [?10]`), which is the honest
  answer when it cannot read.
- **Printed pages**: the typical error is one letter in a long word (`appaṭiyeṉṟūl` for `appaṭiyeṉṟāl`,
  `imbattēṭṭu` for `aimpatteṭṭu`) plus headers and page numbers the reference omits.
- **Weathered synthetic plates**: when an erosion patch covers letters, the reader writes a gap (good) but sometimes
  also guesses neighbouring letters wrong (`svasti śrī kō [?3] ya` for `svasti śrī kō-vicaiya`).

## Changes after the QA review (same day)

The numbers above are the live runs as measured. After review the Reader changed in four ways; their effect was
replayed from the LLM cache with **no new live calls**:

- **Warnings recomputed.** The committed `*.jsonl` files were produced before the Tamil-Brahmi/Grantha/Vatteluttu
  warning existed. `python -m ezhuthu.eval.ocr rewarn` re-ran the current Reader on each record's own cached reply
  (same model, same reading, asserted) and rewrote `warnings` and the new `warning_codes`: the 3 real Jambai
  images now carry `low_reliability_script` (as the app shows them), and 16 routed synthetic records changed.
- **Keep the more confident reading.** Escalation used to prefer Opus's answer whenever it had lines; it now keeps
  the higher-confidence reading (a tie keeps Sonnet's). Replaying the routed runs from the cache under this rule
  changes 7 of 84 synthetic readings: Tamil-script CER 0.097 to 0.100 (3 weathered plates go back to Sonnet's
  reading), Grantha 0.558 to 0.570. On the real Jambai images the mean CER moves from 0.857 to 0.838, but that replay
  also uses Opus replies cached by the Opus-only Brahmi run, so 2 images were escalated that were not in the live
  run. Neither direction is a measurable change. The rule stops a less confident second opinion from silently
  replacing the first; it does not stop invented readings: Opus's invented Jambai phrase (confidence 0.25) beat
  Sonnet's letter salad (0.15) and is kept under either rule. That is why the script warning is shown whatever the
  confidence.
- **Time budget.** Escalation is now gated on the time left in a 25 s request budget (it starts only with 15 s left),
  and every model call, including retries and back-off, is bounded by what is left, so a slow pair of calls ends in
  the first reading rather than a proxy 504. A failed escalation keeps the first reading
  (`escalation_skipped: "error"`).
- **Threshold provenance (DATA_CARD rule 4).** The reader's warning thresholds (`LOW_CONFIDENCE = 0.5`, and the list
  of low-reliability scripts) were chosen while looking at these results, which are on **TEST** lines; the data card
  says thresholds are tuned on VAL. They only decide which caution the app shows, not any restoration, and the
  Brahmi/Grantha decision rests mainly on the 3 real images, but it is still a test-set choice. Re-deriving them on
  VAL plates needs about 30 live calls and is left as a follow-up; until then treat the 0.5 cut as a fixed UX
  setting, not a tuned one. (The routing threshold `routing.escalate_if_confidence_below` comes from
  `config/models.yaml` and was not tuned here.)

## What this does and does not show

- Synthetic plates use modern font letter forms on a texture. They test reading through blur, noise, perspective and
  wear, not reading Pallava or Chola letter shapes; real 1,000-year-old letters are harder, as the Tamil-Brahmi result
  shows.
- Real inscriptions: 3 images of 1 short text. That is all we found that is openly licensed **and** has a published
  reading we could cite. It is enough to say Tamil-Brahmi fails; it is not enough to say anything about Chola stone.
- No Vatteluttu was measured.
- All confidences are the model's own (not calibrated). Escalation thresholds come from `config/models.yaml`.

Claim the app and README may make: **"Photo reading is experimental: CER 0.10 on 72 synthetic Tamil-script plates
(0.05 unless weathered), 0.09 on 3 printed Tamil pages, and it does not read Tamil-Brahmi (CER 0.86 on 3 real
images)."**
